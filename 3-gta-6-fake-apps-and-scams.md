GTA 6 has a huge audience and an expensive price, which gives scammers an easy hook. Two security reports describe what is going around.

## What Malwarebytes found

Malwarebytes reported on November 18 that scammers are using the launch to push fake free-game offers, pre-order codes, discounted keys and map downloads ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/scams/2026/11/gta-6-scams-could-cost-you-your-account-money-or-crypto)). Examples it describes:

- **A "free GTA 6" page that asks you to sign in with Google.** The button loads a copy of Google's sign-in page on the scam site. The giveaway is the address bar, which still shows the scam domain.
- **"Free pre-order code" generators.** Visitors are told to complete a questionnaire and "sponsored offers". Malwarebytes says the page sends people to affiliate tracking links, and the promised code never arrives.
- **Fake Android downloads, crypto token promotions and wallet-connect requests.**

## What BforeAI found on Google Play

BforeAI reported in early October, before launch, that it found 16 unauthorized Android apps on Google Play using GTA 6's name and look to market supposed companion apps, maps, guides, cheat-code tools and countdowns ([Business Computing Journal](https://businesscomputingjournal.com/bforeai-gta-6-unauthorized-apps-google-play/)). Some offered in-app purchases for features that could not work. BforeAI did not call the apps a confirmed malware campaign. It described a low-cost, repeated publishing pattern. Its advice: treat any GTA 6 mobile, map, cheat or companion app as unofficial unless Rockstar says otherwise, check the publisher name, and avoid purchases tied to unreleased features.

## A short checklist

1. **Never enter your Google password on a page that is not google.com.** Look at the address bar, not the tab title.
2. **Skip "free code" and "free key" sites.** If it asks for surveys, offers or a wallet connection, leave.
3. **Check the publisher** on any app listing, and read what it says about affiliation with Rockstar.
4. **Be wary of purchases** for maps, cheats or guides that promise things the listing does not explain.
5. **Prefer apps that name their sources.** A guide or cheat list with dated, named sources can be checked. One without can't.
